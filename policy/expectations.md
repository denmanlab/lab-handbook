# Expectations

The Denman Lab general expectations are:

 - provide good faith effort at all times
 - be honest
 - communicate openly
 - be supportive
 - share. with everyone, in the lab, in the department, at the university, in the scientific community
 - finish what you start

Also, be excited! if your project no longer tickles your scientific fancy, see the last bullet above, and then find a new one!
<br><br>
More detailed expectations are:
<br>
**Support**
<br>Research is an intellectually challenging and often personally difficult process. All members of the lab are expected to be supportive of each other intellectually and emotionally. 
<br><br>
**Work Ethic**
<br>
Discovery, and indeed all of the scientific endeavor, is hard work. This is true of *all* phases of research: in the lab, at the command line, with the (figurative) pen. All members of the lab are expected to devote their full effort to their work. Effective time management is important; so is perseverance. It is expected that all members remain engaged with the lab and their work, and give a good faith effort at all times. 
<br><br>

**Lab Hours and Absences**
<br>
The lab does not have hours. Science does not sleep. But you, lab member, do need to sleep. All members are expected to work a minimum number of hours per week, dependent on your position. All members are also trusted to determine appropriate work hours for their research. Working hours should be devoted to lab-related work. It is expected that at least some working hours for all members occur during normal operating times (9AM-5PM, M-F). 

You are also encouraged and expected to take time away from the lab - vacation, personal, mental health days. The reasons for a short (one day) absence need not be made public, but **any** absence should be on the Denman Lab shared calendar as soon as it is scheduled. Planned longer absences should be discussed with Dan at least two weeks beforehand, and again added to the shared calendar. 
<br><br>

**Communication**
<br>
Multiple channels of communication are critical to the success of an any group. Within the lab, communication may occur through email, Slack, or various other direct means. For many day-to-day tasks, Slack is the most effective form of communication. Lab members are expected to communicate with each other (and Dan), and also with the greater scientific community (see **Dissemination** below). Communication should always be honest. Responses from all members, even senior, are expected to be timely. If you feel as if there is a communication breakdown, try another method and/or involve Dan. 
<br><br>

**Dissemination**
<br>
The lab expects to disseminate all of the work done in the lab. As such, all work is expected to be sufficiently documented for publication or other public sharing. 

When preparing to disseminate (in any form: code sharing, pre-print posting, manuscript submission, presentation), it is expected that Dan will have sufficient time to review the materials before dissemination, and that he will provide timely, constructive feedback. 
<br><br>

**Career Development**
<br>
All members are expected, on joining, to develop an [individual development plan (IDP)][1] with Dan. Even if this exercise feels silly or foreign, please consider it seriously, as it will be a useful tool for setting expectations in the mentor-mentee relationship. IDPs will be reviewed and revised at least annually. 

All members are expected to meet regularly with the lab (attend lab meetings) and with Dan. The frequency of these individual  meetings will be determined in the development of the written IDP.
